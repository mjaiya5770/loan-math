How to Pay Off a Car Loan Early (and What It Actually Saves)

Extra payments on a car loan go straight to principal — but how much do they save? On a $28,000 loan at 6%, $100 a month extra ends the loan 10 months early and saves $810.45. The mechanics, the numbers, and the two checks to run first.

How-to · Published November 8, 2026 · Loan Math

Estimates only — not financial advice. Rates, taxes and fees vary by lender and state; confirm every figure with your lender before borrowing.

The mechanism: why early money beats late money

A car loan’s scheduled payment is mostly interest early on. The lender calculates interest on the remaining balance each month, and because the balance is largest at the start, the first payments carry the heaviest interest load. Every extra dollar you send skips that interest entirely and cuts the principal directly. From then on, every later month’s interest is charged on a smaller balance. That is why early money beats late money: a dollar of principal removed in month two works much harder than a dollar removed in month fifty.

Before you send extra money: two checks

Make these checks before the first extra payment:

  • Does the loan carry a prepayment penalty? A prepayment penalty is uncommon on mainstream auto loans, but it exists — the contract answers this, not the internet. Look for a prepayment, early payoff, or finance charge rebate clause.
  • Is the extra payment being applied to principal? Tell the servicer “principal only.” An unmarked extra payment can be held against future payments instead of reducing principal. If that happens, you are not saving the interest you think you are.
Principal only is a payment instruction, not a preference. Check the lender’s process online or call before you send the money. A few seconds of instruction changes the entire result.

The lump-sum variant

One early lump sum does the same job as a stream of smaller extras. A single principal payment in the first quarter of the loan removes a large block of balance before it can accrue months of interest. Earlier is stronger than bigger-then-late: $2,000 applied in month three usually saves more interest than $2,000 applied in month thirty. The honest caveats

  • Prepaid money is locked in the car. Principal paid ahead is not sitting in a savings account. It reduces the loan balance, but it is not liquid cash you can pull back out.
  • An emergency buffer comes first. Extra payments should not empty the cash you need for a repair, medical bill, or income gap. A paid-down car loan does not pay an emergency expense.
  • Higher-rate debt beats car debt. If the same dollars could erase a higher-rate balance elsewhere, the expensive debt is the better target. That ordering question is a rates comparison, not a loyalty question.

The 72-84 month term connection

Extra payments matter so much on car loans because the modern 72–84 month term stretches the same balance over more months, which raises total interest even when the payment looks smaller. Our dealer guide’s numbers show the effect: the same $25,000 at 8% over 72 months costs $6,559.83 of interest against $5,056.92 at 7.5% over 60. Extras are how you claw a stretched term back. Frequently asked questions

Does paying extra lower my monthly bill?

No. The required payment stays the same. Extra payments shorten the term, unless you refinance or the lender offers a recast.

Is a bigger down payment the same as extra payments?

They have a similar interest effect but different timing. Money down never accrues interest at all; money extra stops it early. Both beat money paid late.

What if I can only add extra some months?

Send it when you have it. The mechanism does not require consistency, only earliness.
